IPO GMP (grey market premium), explained

BrokerLens does not publish GMP figures. A grey market premium is a price agreed in private, off-exchange deals before an IPO lists. No exchange or regulator records those deals, so a quoted GMP cannot be checked against anything, and different sources often quote different numbers for the same issue.

What to look at instead

  • Subscription status: how many times each category of an open issue has been bid for, from the exchange's own disclosures. This is the official measure of demand.
  • Upcoming IPOs: price bands and bidding dates as the exchange lists them.
  • Allotment status: how to check whether you were allotted shares with the registrar or the exchange.
  • IPO tracker: issue price, listing date and, where the price history on file covers it, the listing-day price of past issues.

Frequently asked questions about IPO GMP

What is IPO Grey Market Premium (GMP)?

Grey Market Premium (GMP) is an unofficial, off-exchange rate at which IPO applications or shares are traded between private parties before the shares list. A positive GMP is read as buyers expecting a listing above the issue price; a negative one as soft demand.

Does GMP guarantee listing day profits?

No. GMP has no regulatory oversight and can move sharply before listing. The listing price is set by the exchange's pre-open call auction on the listing morning.

What is the difference between Kostak rate and GMP?

GMP is a premium quoted per share. A kostak rate is a fixed amount offered for a whole retail application before allotment, whether or not the application is allotted shares.

Why does BrokerLens not show GMP figures?

Grey-market deals are private and are not reported to any exchange or to SEBI, so there is no record a quoted figure can be checked against. BrokerLens publishes only data it can trace to a source. For demand, use the exchange's own subscription figures.
